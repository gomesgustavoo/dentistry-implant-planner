A model for your structures

The taxonomy this service ships is 47 structures, and it is somebody else’s taxonomy — ToothFairy3’s, plus a specialist for the anterior canals. If the class you need is not in it, or your annotation protocol differs from theirs, the answer is a model trained on your data rather than a relabelling of this one.

What that takes

  • Annotated cases. Below about 40 there is nothing to hold back for a holdout, and without a holdout no accuracy claim about the result is measurable — which is the one thing this service does that its competitors do not.
  • One protocol, written down. Where a structure starts and stops. Two annotators disagreeing by 1 mm is a 1 mm error budget you can never get back.
  • A held-out split you do not touch. The numbers on this site — strict Dice 0.8292, HD95 1.235 mm, challenge Dice 0.8965 — are 20 cases the model never saw.
  • Time on a GPU. The production model is an nnU-Net with a U-Mamba2 bottleneck; a fine-tune from these weights is days, not hours.

What this is not

Not a medical device, and not for diagnostic use. The segmentation weights here are ToothFairy3-derived and carry CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — research and non-commercial use only — so a model trained from them inherits that, and a commercially usable model has to start from data and weights that allow it. That is a licensing question before it is a technical one, and it is worth settling first.

Get in touch

    Bring the structure list and roughly how many annotated cases you have. Those two decide whether the answer is a fine-tune, a new class on top of this taxonomy, or that there is not enough data yet — and the third answer is a real one.

    Credits & scope

    Segmentation by a single model of our own: an U-Mamba2 (Wu et al., MICCAI 2025 — first place in both ToothFairy3 tasks) finetuned on the ToothFairy3 challenge dataset (University of Modena and Reggio Emilia), trained here on 512 CBCT volumes and built on nnU-Net (Isensee et al., Nature Methods 2021).

    The model inherits the ToothFairy3 dataset licence, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: attribution, share-alike, and non-commercial use only.

    Cases segmented before 2026-08-25 came from two published checkpoints we no longer run, both CC BY 4.0, and their downloads are still derived from those weights: DentalSegmentator (Dot et al.) and ToothSeg (van Nistelrooij, Krämer, Isensee et al.). Neither author endorses this service.

    Research preview — not a medical device, and not for diagnostic use. CBCT grey values are not calibrated Hounsfield units, and these scans have no ground truth: every number in the quality panel is a claim about internal consistency, not about accuracy.
