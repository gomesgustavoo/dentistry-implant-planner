What comes back

Four files, not a screenshot

A segmentation you cannot load into the software you already use is a demo. Everything here is written in the coordinate frame you uploaded.

  • RTSTRUCT

    An RT Structure Set with one ROI per structure. ROI names are capped at 16 characters so they load in Varian Eclipse, and checked for collisions at that length. Round-tripped through an independent reader: 34 ROIs, median Dice 0.9920. Upload NIfTI instead of DICOM and a DERIVED\SECONDARY CT series ships in the same zip — an RTSTRUCT that references nothing will not load in any planning system.

  • STL

    One mesh per structure, in patient LPS millimetres, Taubin smoothed and quadric decimated. Iso-surfaced from a smoothed field rather than a smoothed label map, so no thin cortical wall is ever pinched apart.

  • NIfTI

    The multi-label map, on the grid you uploaded — not the canonical frame the network ran in. Re-export the same scan as LPS, RPI, RAS or PIR and the labels come back 100.000000% identical.

  • Viewer

    Three synced planes plus a 3D pane in the browser, with a clickable FDI chart: pick a tooth and every view jumps to its centroid. Centroids are computed in-browser and agree with SimpleITK to 0.0000 mm.

The high-stakes one

The canal gets its own colour

The inferior alveolar nerve is the structure you plan an implant around. It is never merged into the mandible, and it is the one thing on screen in red. Two models draw the nerves between them: the base model draws the inferior alveolar canal itself, and a second, purpose-trained model owns the three accessory canals — the two incisive canals and the lingual canal — taking the main canal only as context.

Measured, without labels

0.11 mm from the image edge

The exported surface sits 0.11 mm inside where the image intensity crosses half-way — 23% of one voxel, below the scan's own point-spread function, over 25,487 sampled points. That is a precision number that needs no ground truth.

Thin things fragment

Reported, never repaired

A canal legitimately comes out in two pieces, so small-island removal is switched off for it alone. Nothing is cleaned up behind your back: the component count is in the report.

How it works

One model, and everything it will not tell you

A single network labels all 47 structures, including a class per tooth. What it cannot do is mark its own mistakes, so the checks are separate and they are all in the report.

  1. Step 01

    Into one known frame

    Teeth are numbered by position, so a scan in the wrong frame gets every tooth mirrored. Every upload is canonicalised before a voxel is read, including scans with a real patient tilt.

  2. Step 02

    All 47 structures at once

    Both jaws, the mandibular canal, the maxillary sinuses, the pharynx, the accessory canals, dental work, and a separate class for every FDI position — from a single network, in about 20 s on one GPU.

  3. Step 03

    Fragments removed, per class

    A speck of enamel is not a tooth. Each class is filtered against the smallest that structure has ever legitimately been — except the canals, which are thin tubes that come out in pieces and would be deleted by their own filter.

  4. Check

    Checked against anatomy, not against itself

    Per-tooth component counts, contralateral volume symmetry, occlusal contact, four laterality checks and an upper/lower check — all in the report, whether they pass or not.

Before you trust it

What we do not claim

  • None of these numbers are accuracy. The example cases are public sample data with no ground truth. "29 of 32 teeth" is how many labels the model produced, not how many it got right.
  • The checks are consistency, not correctness. Symmetry, laterality and component counts catch gross failure and say nothing about whether tooth 26 is really tooth 26. There is no ground truth on a patient scan, so nothing here is an accuracy figure.
  • The same scan twice is not bit-identical. Three runs of one file differ by 108–185 voxels out of 42.8 million — ordinary GPU non-determinism, almost all of it single voxels on a soft-tissue boundary. No numbered tooth differs by more than one voxel, but do not quote these figures to four significant digits.
  • CBCT grey values are not calibrated Hounsfield units. Anything that looks like a density measurement is not one.
  • Nothing is repaired. Quality findings — extra components, a tooth sitting in the wrong arch, asymmetric contralateral volumes — are reported. The exported label map is exactly what the models produced.
  • Research preview. Not a medical device, not for diagnostic use. A clinician reads every result.

Reference

Questions worth asking

What can I upload?

A DICOM series as a .zip, or a single-file volume: .nii / .nii.gz. Upload DICOM if you can — the RTSTRUCT then references your own series, which is what makes it drop straight into a planning system.

There is a hard 100 MB ceiling per upload at our edge, which a 0.25 mm full-head CBCT can exceed. Chunked upload for larger scans is not built yet, and the app tells you before it wastes your time rather than after.

Whose models are these?

Ours, finetuned from someone else’s. The architecture is U-Mamba2 (Wu et al., MICCAI 2025), which won both ToothFairy3 tasks; we finetuned it on the ToothFairy3 challenge dataset — 512 annotated CBCT volumes from the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia — and that checkpoint is what runs here. The service used to run two published models instead; it does not any more.

Because the training data is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so is the model: attribution, share-alike, and non-commercial use only. This is a research preview and is licensed as one.

Does the orientation of my scan matter?

No, and this is worth being specific about because it is the failure everyone hits. The model numbers teeth by position, so a scan in the wrong frame gets every tooth mirrored — 11 becomes 21, left becomes right. Every upload is canonicalised first, including scans with a real patient tilt. Re-export the same scan as LPS, RPI, RAS or PIR and the labels come back 100.000000% identical over 4.8 million voxels.

How long does a scan take?

54 to 93 seconds of GPU time on the seeded example cases, plus roughly a minute of CPU for the merge, the quality checks, the meshes and the viewer tiles. There is one GPU, so a job may queue behind another; the app shows you where you are.

What happens to my data?

The upload is deleted the moment a job reaches done — it is never read again. Results expire 72 hours after completion and the endpoint then answers 410 Gone rather than a 404 that would read like a bug. Each account's cases are stored under their own prefix and no account can see another's.

Can I trust it for planning?

Treat it as a first pass that a clinician checks, which is what the limits section is for. It is a research preview, not a medical device, and it is not for diagnostic use. The useful question is not whether it is perfect but whether checking its canal is faster than tracing one — and the report tells you where it is unsure.

What counts as one segmentation?

One scan through the full pipeline, with every export produced. Re-running the same scan counts again. A job that fails does not count, and a job you cancel before it reaches the GPU does not count either.
